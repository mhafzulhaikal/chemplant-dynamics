Stirred Tank Heater (STHR) — Process Overview

Dynamic Thermal & Mass Balance Simulation Model

Process Control Objective
Regulate liquid outlet temperature ($T$) and hold-up level ($H$) under varying inlet feed disturbances ($F_{in}, T_{in}$).

1. System Summary

The Stirred Tank Heater is a classic chemical engineering unit operation where cold inlet fluid is thoroughly mixed and heated via steam coil tubes.

  • TIC-101 (Temperature Loop): Manipulates steam inlet control valve ($V_s$).
  • LIC-101 (Level Loop): Manipulates discharge drain valve ($V_o$).

2. Dynamic Model Equations

2.1 Liquid Level Mass Balance

The rate of change of liquid height $H$ with cross-sectional area $A$:

$$A \frac{dH}{dt} = F_{in} - F_{out}$$

Where gravity outlet discharge $F_{out}$ follows Torricelli flow characteristics:

$$F_{out} = K_v \cdot V_o \cdot \sqrt{\rho g H}$$

2.2 Thermal Energy Balance

Assuming perfect mixing and negligible wall heat capacitance:

$$\rho C_p V \frac{dT}{dt} = \rho C_p F_{in} (T_{in} - T) + Q_{steam} - Q_{loss}$$

The steam heating rate $Q_{steam}$ is governed by heat transfer surface area $A_s$:

$$Q_{steam} = U A_s \cdot (T_{steam} - T) \cdot V_s$$

Biodiesel Transesterification Reactor

Kinetic Rate & Thermal Energy Balance Model

Reaction Chemistry
Transesterification of Triglycerides ($\text{TG}$) with Methanol ($\text{MeOH}$) using basic catalyst ($\text{NaOH}$) yields Fatty Acid Methyl Esters ($\text{FAME}$) and Glycerol byproduct ($\text{GL}$).

1. Process Description

The continuous stirred tank reactor maintains optimal reaction kinetics while removing exothermic heat via water cooling jacket coils.

$$\text{Triglyceride (TG)} + 3\,\text{MeOH} \xrightarrow{\text{Catalyst}} 3\,\text{Biodiesel (FAME)} + \text{Glycerol (GL)}$$

2. Reaction Kinetics & Energy Model

2.1 Arrhenius Rate Equation

$$r_A = k_0 \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{R T}\right) \cdot C_{TG} \cdot C_{MeOH}^3$$

2.2 Mass & Thermal Energy Balance

$$V \frac{dC_{TG}}{dt} = F_{in} C_{TG,in} - F_{out} C_{TG} - V \cdot r_A$$
$$\rho C_p V \frac{dT}{dt} = \rho C_p F_{in}(T_{in} - T) + (-\Delta H_{rxn}) \cdot V \cdot r_A + U A_{jacket} (T_{jacket} - T) \cdot V_{cool}$$

Process Control Theory & PID Tuning

Mathematical Foundations of Feedback Control Loops

1. Continuous PID Controller Algorithm

The standard parallel continuous-time PID algorithm in ideal gain form is defined as:

$$u(t) = K_p e(t) + K_i \int_{0}^{t} e(\tau) d\tau + K_d \frac{de(t)}{dt}$$

Where $u(t)$ is the controller output ($0-100\%$) and $e(t) = SP - PV(t)$ represents the instantaneous control error.

2. Discrete Velocity Form (Anti-Windup)

For discrete numerical simulation and microcontroller implementation, the algorithm is expressed in velocity form to prevent integral windup:

$$\Delta u_k = K_p \left( e_k - e_{k-1} \right) + K_p \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_i} e_k + K_p \frac{\tau_d}{\Delta t} \left( PV_k - 2 PV_{k-1} + PV_{k-2} \right)$$
$$u_k = u_{k-1} + \Delta u_k$$

ANSI/ISA-101.01 High Performance HMI Guidelines

Standardized Human Factors & Situational Awareness Engineering

Color Discipline in High Performance HMI
Color is reserved strictly for drawing operator attention to abnormal upset states. Static background elements must remain neutral dark gray/black tones.

1. Four Levels of HMI Displays

  • Level 1: Overview of entire plant / operating area.
  • Level 2: Unit Operation P&ID graphic display.
  • Level 3: Detailed equipment faceplates and diagnostic tuning modals.
  • Level 4: Sub-system diagnostics, interlocks, and maintenance procedures.
